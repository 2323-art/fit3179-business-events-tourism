Reading both charts

The scatterplot shows the selected year. Higher points indicate greater spending for similar trip volumes. Year and scale controls affect only the scatterplot. The heatmap compares 2024–2025 throughout: purple indicates increases; orange indicates decreases.

Hovering or selecting a market links both views. Smaller markets enlarges the cluster with an overview for context. Axes stay fixed across years within each scale. Small changes may reflect survey variability. Spending is not inflation-adjusted. Heatmap rows follow average change across three measures, not overall performance.

Conclusion

Visitor numbers alone give an incomplete picture of Australia’s business-event tourism. Domestic overnight trips generate most spending, while regional spending shares differ between states. Malaysia’s visitor growth outpaced its spending growth, showing that more trips need not bring a similar rise in spending. Together, visitor volume, spending per trip and where spending occurs provide a fuller picture of tourism’s value.

What the numbers mean

AUD = Australian dollars; m = million; bn = billion. Values are rounded survey estimates, without inflation adjustment.

Coverage, comparability and missing values
  • Domestic estimates form a 2025 snapshot. Australia changed its domestic survey in January 2025; earlier domestic values are not directly comparable. Year-to-year spending comparisons here use international visitors.
  • Trips are not unique people. Multi-state trips count in each visited state.
  • Visitor totals combine attendees and accompanying visitors without counting overlapping groups twice. Activity rates cover attendees only.
  • Surveys exclude most local attendees and some industry expenditure. Visitor spending is not total economic impact.
  • Missing or withheld estimates are not zero. No uncertainty ranges are supplied; small changes may reflect survey variability.
  • Regional totals exclude AUD 1m of other/unknown spending and zero/unallocated entries. Rounding can produce differences from state totals.
  • Spending per resident combines tourism spending and population estimates. It measures relative scale, not residents’ benefit.
