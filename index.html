A trip for a meeting can become a hotel stay, a dinner out and a day exploring. Where does that value land?

In 2025, business-event visitors spent A$17.2 billion in Australia. But the travellers who made the most trips were not the ones who generated the most spending.

Follow the visitor dollar ↓
What counts?

Visitors who attend—or accompany someone attending—a corporate meeting, conference, convention, seminar, exhibition or trade fair. These estimates cover spending during the whole trip, not just at the event. Most local attendees are outside the tourism surveys.

01

The value of a visit

Staying longer changes the equation.

Domestic overnight visitors are the foundation of the market. International visitors are fewer, but spend much more per trip.

A break in the domestic series

Australia changed its domestic tourism survey in January 2025. The method and coverage changed, so this story uses domestic data as a 2025 snapshot. All year-to-year comparisons below concern international visitors, measured by the International Visitor Survey.

Australia's international connections

Close neighbours. Uneven momentum.

International visitor trips fell 6.7% to about 731,000 in 2025. Spending fell 6.9%, but that national change hides very different origin markets.

What to take away

Look beyond attendance.

For a Malaysian reader considering Australia’s events market, visitor numbers are only the starting point. Domestic overnight stays sustain most spending; smaller territories have a different population-adjusted footprint; and international origins move in different directions. Hotels, meals and exploration belong in the story alongside the event itself.

Read estimates as estimates

  • All money is nominal Australian dollars (AUD). “m” means million and “b” means billion. No exchange-rate or inflation adjustment is applied.
  • Visitor counts describe trips/visits, not distinct people. State visits can count multi-state trips more than once.
  • Use the published union of attendance and accompanying visitors: these overlapping subgroups must not be added together.
  • The surveys exclude most local attendees and some event-industry expenditure. Visitor spending is not the total economic impact of business events.
  • Published values are rounded and subject to survey error. Suppressed or unavailable values are not treated as zero. No confidence intervals are supplied in the extracted tables; small changes should be interpreted cautiously.
  • Geographic spending per resident combines independently estimated tourism and population statistics. It is a scale comparison, not a measure of residents’ benefit.
