Beyond theConference Badge

Exploring Australia's business-event visitors, destinations and spending in 2025

A business-events field guide

A trip for a meeting can become a hotel stay, a dinner out and a day exploring. Where does that value land?

In 2025, business-event visitors spent AUD 17.2 billion in Australia. But the travellers who made the most trips were not the ones who generated the most spending.

Follow the visitor dollar ↓

What counts?

Visitors who attend—or accompany someone attending—a business meeting, corporate event, conference, convention, seminar, exhibition or trade fair. These estimates cover spending during the whole trip, not just at the event. Most local attendees are outside the tourism surveys. All amounts are in Australian dollars (AUD); “bn” means billion and “m” means million.

01

The value of a visit

Staying longer changes the equation.

Domestic overnight visitors are the foundation of the market. International visitors are fewer, but spend much more per trip.

A break in the domestic series

Australia changed its domestic tourism survey in January 2025. The method and coverage changed, so this story uses domestic data as a 2025 snapshot. All year-to-year comparisons below concern international visitors, measured by the International Visitor Survey.

Where the value lands

Big markets. Different local significance.

New South Wales leads in visitor volume. Relative to population, the Northern Territory and the Australian Capital Territory stand out.

03

Australia's international connections

Close neighbours. Uneven momentum.

International visitor trips fell 6.7% to about 731,000 in 2025. Spending fell 6.9%, but that national change hides very different origin markets.

Beyond the meeting room

The visitor is also a diner and explorer.

Business events connect to the wider visitor economy. The trip continues after the conference badge comes off.

What to take away

Look beyond attendance.

For a Malaysian reader considering Australia’s events market, visitor numbers are only the starting point. Domestic overnight stays sustain most spending; smaller territories have a different population-adjusted footprint; and international origins move in different directions. Hotels, meals and exploration belong in the story alongside the event itself.

Read estimates as estimates

  • All money is in Australian dollars at the prices of each year. Amounts have not been converted to another currency or adjusted for inflation.
  • Visitor counts describe trips/visits, not distinct people. State visits can count multi-state trips more than once.
  • The combined visitor total counts those who attended an event or accompanied someone attending, without counting a trip twice if it belongs to both groups.
  • The surveys exclude most local attendees and some event-industry expenditure. Visitor spending is not the total economic impact of business events.
  • Published values are rounded survey estimates. Values withheld or unavailable in the source are not treated as zero. The supplied tables do not give a range for the uncertainty in these estimates, so small changes may not reflect a real change in travel behaviour.
  • Regional totals exclude AUD 1m of other/unknown spending and zero/unallocated entries. Published rounding means regional and state totals may differ slightly.
  • Geographic spending per resident combines independently estimated tourism and population statistics. It is a scale comparison, not a measure of residents’ benefit.
