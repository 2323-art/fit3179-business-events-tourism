Who counts as a business-event visitor?

Visitors who attend—or accompany someone attending—a business meeting, corporate event, conference, convention, seminar, exhibition or trade fair. These estimates cover spending during the whole trip, not just at the event. Most local attendees are outside the tourism surveys.

Which visits generate the most spending?

Overnight domestic trips generate most spending in total. International trips generate the most per trip. These are different ways to describe value.

A break in the domestic series

Australia changed its domestic tourism survey in January 2025. The method and coverage changed, so this story uses domestic data as a 2025 snapshot. All year-to-year comparisons below concern international visitors, measured by the International Visitor Survey.

What happens beyond the meeting room?

Visitor spending covers the whole trip. Activity participation shows what attendees do alongside the event; it does not establish when activities happened or whether the event caused them.

What to take away

Look beyond attendance.

Visitor numbers are the starting point, not the whole story. Overnight domestic trips dominate spending; state traveller mixes and regional shares differ. Malaysia’s trips rose 31%, but spending per trip fell 21%, leaving spending only 4% higher. Event visitors still spent more per night than other international visitors, although that gap narrowed. For Malaysian readers, the useful question is who visits, how long they stay and what they spend—not simply which destination ranks first.

Read estimates as estimates

  • All money is in Australian dollars at the prices of each year. Amounts have not been converted to another currency or adjusted for inflation.
  • Visitor counts describe trips/visits, not distinct people. State visits can count multi-state trips more than once.
  • The combined visitor total counts those who attended an event or accompanied someone attending, without counting a trip twice if it belongs to both groups.
  • The surveys exclude most local attendees and some event-industry expenditure. Visitor spending is not the total economic impact of business events.
  • Published values are rounded survey estimates. Values withheld or unavailable in the source are not treated as zero. The supplied tables do not give a range for the uncertainty in these estimates, so small changes may not reflect a real change in travel behaviour.
  • Regional totals exclude AUD 1m of other/unknown spending and zero/unallocated entries. Published rounding means regional and state totals may differ slightly.
  • Geographic spending per resident combines independently estimated tourism and population statistics. It is a scale comparison, not a measure of residents’ benefit.
