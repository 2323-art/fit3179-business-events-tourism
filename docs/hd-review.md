## Section-by-section contribution and sufficiency

| Section | Question and unique contribution | Reuse versus new evidence | Remaining limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening / Value (01–03) | Why are most trips not most spending? Shares establish the mismatch; per-trip rates make its scale readable; the Sankey defines how much international expenditure occurs within Australia. | 01 and 02 use the same trips/spending, deliberately shown as composition then a useful ratio. Nights adds duration context; total trip spend adds a new spending-location scope in 03. | Aggregate rates do not prove that duration causes spending; no domestic growth comparison crosses the 2025 survey break. |
| Places (04–06 plus state mix) | Where are visits concentrated, how large is spending relative to residents, and does traveller composition vary within a place? The treemap reveals regional shares hidden by state totals. | ABS residents add a genuinely external denominator; state × traveller rows add a categorical dimension to the map; region leaves add spatial resolution. The map pair changes measures, rather than isolating one denominator effect. | Per-resident spending is not household benefit, dependence or causal impact. State visits overlap across trips; rounded components can differ from separate totals. Source regions are not a uniform city/rural classification. |
| International origins (07–10) | Does a larger market necessarily generate more spending, and does trip growth imply spending growth? Geographic reach supplies orientation; the scatter compares volume/value; the heatmap adds nights and relative change; the waterfall contrasts absolute contribution and national coverage. | 07 repeats the origin visitor counts geographically; 08 introduces their spending relationship; 09 re-expresses changes from the same market records and adds the night measure. The linked rate explanation is an accounting identity, not independent causal evidence. 10 adds the national total and residual coverage. | Two years establish a change, not a sustained trend. Published markets do not identify the origins behind 77.6% of the net decline. Neither geographic proximity nor arithmetic rates explain causes. |
| Whole-trip behaviour / conclusion (11–12) | What do event attendees do during their trips, and how does spending intensity compare with other international tourists? Activities add behaviour; the per-night view adds a different comparator and exposes the narrowing premium. | Activity participation is a new variable and attendee-only population. Other international visitors are a new comparison group; 12 is not a duration-adjusted version of 02. | No observed timing relative to the event, tracking of the same people, or proof that attending caused activity/spending. The premium uses rounded annual averages in nominal AUD. |

The existing data are sufficient for these scoped questions and the assignment's multi-variable, time-comparison and two-source requirements. Adding event counts, event types or a longer historical series would answer different questions; their absence is not itself an actionable defect. Effective presentation and independent evaluation remain necessary: these data do not guarantee an HD.

| 01 · Waffle | Day trips dominate trip counts; domestic overnight trips dominate Australian spending. | Establishes the central volume/value mismatch through shares of the same national whole. | Whole-cell rounding requires the exact numeric key. It does not explain why the shares differ. |
| 02 · Per-trip bars | International event trips generate more spending per trip, while average stay lengths differ greatly. | Expresses the opening contrast as an accessible rate and supplies nights/trip context. | **Same trips/spending aggregates as 01**, not independent evidence. | 05 · Symbol map | New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland have the largest visit volumes. | Locates the absolute visitation distribution on familiar jurisdiction geography. | The ranking alone is descriptive; its place in the argument comes from contrast with 04 and the linked traveller mix. | 06 · Treemap | Regional New South Wales contributes a larger share of displayed state spending than Regional Victoria. | Adds sub-state detail and a within-state comparison that national/state totals conceal. | City dollar rankings overlap the previous geography story; regional shares carry the additional finding. | 07 · Flow map | International connections span New Zealand, Asia, Europe and North America; the displayed Asian markets and New Zealand account for substantial trip shares. | Makes origin geography explicit for a general Malaysian readership. | **Uses the same origin trip counts as 08** for a different spatial question. | 08 · Scatterplot | The market with most trips is not necessarily the market with most spending. | Compares two attributes simultaneously and exposes differing spending per trip; fixed scales support the two-year comparison. | Reuses 07’s count measure, adding expenditure and the broader published-market set. The optional detail omits out-of-bounds markets explicitly; its controls are not additional evidence. |
| 09 · Heatmap | Trips, nights and spending can change in different directions or by different amounts. | Compares relative changes across three measures and all 18 published origins. | Derived from the same market observations as 08, not a separate dataset. Two years show a change, not a sustained trend; small differences may reflect survey variability. |
| 10 · Waterfall | Named-market gains and losses alone do not account for the national decline; 77.6% is in the unpublished/rounding remainder. | Converts the story from **relative change in 09 to absolute contributions**, incorporating the national total and source coverage. | It reconciles accounting totals; it cannot identify the origins in the remainder or explain why spending changed. A large residual is a disclosed source limit, not evidence against the arithmetic. |
| 11 · Activity slopegraph | Dining participation remains high while sightseeing participation falls between the two years. | Introduces behaviour beyond counts/expenditure and the narrower attendee-only population. | Selected activities are whole-trip, multiple-response estimates, not activity timing, expenditure by activity, or the same visitors followed over time. |
| 12 · Per-night comparison | Event visitors spend more per night than other international visitors, but the premium narrows from 59.4% to 49.5%. | Adds **a different comparator from 02** and a useful two-year intensity comparison. | It does not show that 02’s international/domestic gap survives duration adjustment. Published rounded group averages are not controls for visitor mix or proof of an event effect. |

The repeated measurements above are purposeful where they answer a different reader task. The requirement for varied idioms should not be mistaken for a requirement to manufacture independent datasets or causal explanations. Conversely, chart variety alone does not guarantee a coherent story; the sequence depends on the explicitly stated comparisons and scope changes.
